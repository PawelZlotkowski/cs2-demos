# Fundamentals

General rules the coach can cite on any map. Each section names the detector it explains, so a finding retrieves the matching rule.

## Trading

Detectors: untraded_death, good_plays.
A trade is a kill on the enemy who just killed your teammate, within a few seconds. Trading keeps rounds even: if every death is traded, a team never falls behind in numbers. To be tradeable, stay close enough to a teammate that one of you can swing on the killer at once, but not so close that one spray or grenade hits both.

## Untraded deaths

Detectors: untraded_death.
Dying with no teammate near enough to trade gives the enemy a free man advantage. Common causes are lurking without a plan, pushing ahead of the team, and holding an angle alone that the team cannot reach. If a position cannot be traded, play it for information and fall back after first contact rather than taking a duel.

## Refrag spacing

Detectors: untraded_death, good_plays.
When entering as a pair, the second player follows close enough to see the entry's killer within about a second. Spacing of a few metres lets the second player refrag without both being in the same crosshair. Too far back and the trade comes late; too close and both die to one spray.

## Crossfires

Detectors: untraded_death, dry_peek.
A crossfire is two players watching the same area from different angles, so an enemy who fights one is exposed to the other. Defenders build crossfires on site entrances; attackers break them with smokes and by entering from two directions. Holding an angle alone with no crossfire makes a player easy to isolate.

## Counter-strafing and shooting while moving

Detectors: shot_while_moving.
Rifles, snipers and pistols are only accurate when the player is almost standing still (about a third of the weapon's top speed or less). Counter-strafing means tapping the opposite movement key to stop instantly before shooting. Shots fired while running go wide; if you must move, stop first, shoot, then move again. SMGs and shotguns are more forgiving on the move.

## Spray control and bursts

Detectors: shot_while_moving.
At long range, taps and short bursts are more accurate than a full spray. Full sprays are for close range and for walls of enemies. Resetting between bursts (a short pause) brings the crosshair back to the first-shot accuracy.

## Using utility before dying

Detectors: unused_utility.
Grenades only help if they are thrown. Dying with a smoke, flash or molotov in hand means the team fought with fewer tools than it bought. Before taking a risky duel, use the grenade that makes it safer, or pass it to a teammate who can use it. A molotov kept for the post-plant is the one exception, but only if the player stays alive to use it.

## Dry peeks

Detectors: dry_peek.
A dry peek is taking a fight without any help: no flash, no smoke and no teammate ready to trade. Against a prepared defender, a dry peek is usually a losing duel. Use a flash (your own or a teammate's), a smoke to cut one angle, or peek together so the defender cannot kill both.

## Jiggle and shoulder peeks

Detectors: dry_peek.
To get information without committing, jiggle peek: show a small part of the model briefly and move back. It baits an AWP shot or reveals a position without a real duel. Wide peeks are for when you are ready to fight with help.

## Flashing well

Detectors: team_flash, good_plays.
A good flash pops as the enemy would see it and away from teammates. Pop flashes thrown off a wall or high over a corner blind defenders without warning. Call flashes before throwing them so teammates can turn away. A flash that blinds your own entry player is worse than no flash.

## Team flashes and self flashes

Detectors: team_flash.
Flashing a teammate for more than a second can get them killed and wastes the grenade. Common causes: throwing straight ahead of a teammate who is about to peek, and not calling the flash. If the path of the flash crosses a teammate's view, change the throw or wait until they are past the corner.

## Opening duels

Detectors: opening_duel, good_plays.
The first kill of a round changes the odds most: a five against four is a large edge for the side that got the kill. Opening duels should be taken with an advantage (a flash, a crossfire, an off-angle) by players whose role is to take them. Support players who lose opening duels without help cost the team the round's first advantage.

## Entry fragging

Detectors: good_plays, opening_duel.
The entry fragger goes first into a site to take the first fight and make space. The entry needs utility thrown for them and a trade partner right behind. An entry kill that is followed by a trade on the entry is still a good exchange if the site is taken.

## Economy basics

Detectors: economy_mismatch.
Teams should buy together: all full-buy, all save, or all force. A single player buying rifles while the team saves leaves them alone with good equipment and no support; a single player saving while the team buys leaves the team a player short. Check the team's money before buying and follow the team call.

## Economy numbers

Detectors: economy_mismatch.
Rough CS2 values to reason about buys (check the current patch before quoting exact numbers): a full rifle buy with armour and grenades costs around 5000, a loss bonus that grows with consecutive losses, and a bonus for Ts who plant the bomb even if they lose. Kill rewards are larger for SMGs and shotguns than for rifles, which is why SMGs are common on force buys.

## Saving

Detectors: economy_mismatch, late_rotation.
Saving means avoiding fights to keep weapons for the next round when the current round is lost. A save is correct when the numbers or time make winning very unlikely. Saving in a winnable round, or dying while trying to save, are both costly.

## Rotations and timing

Detectors: late_rotation.
Rotators should move when the enemy commits to a site (several players or a lot of utility), not after the bomb is planted. A late rotation arrives after the site is lost and players come in one at a time. Rotating too early on a fake is the opposite mistake, so good rotations depend on teammates calling what they see.

## Retakes

Detectors: late_rotation.
A retake works when players group and enter together with utility. Walking in one at a time from different directions lets post-plant players kill each retaker alone. Check the time: if there is not enough time to clear the site and defuse, saving is often better.

## Post-plant play

Detectors: good_plays.
After the plant, attackers should play from positions that cross the bomb from different angles, keep utility to stop the defuse, and not take unnecessary duels. Time is on the attackers' side, so the defenders must come to them.

## Varying positions

Detectors: repeated_death_zone.
Dying in the same place several rounds in a row means the enemy has read the position. Change the angle, the timing or the spot, or bring utility to that position. Good players play the same spot differently from round to round.

## Clutches

Detectors: good_plays.
In a clutch (one against several), isolate duels one at a time, use sound and time, and avoid peeking two enemies at once. Playing for the bomb (the plant or the defuse) forces enemies to come to you. Saving is fine when the odds are poor and the economy matters.

## Multi-kills

Detectors: good_plays.
Multi-kills usually come from good positioning: an off-angle, a crossfire, or catching enemies moving through a choke point. When reviewing a multi-kill, look at what set it up, not only the aim.

## Utility damage

Detectors: good_plays.
Molotovs and HE grenades that deal damage before a fight soften enemies so they die to fewer bullets. Stacked HE grenades on a common position can win rounds on their own.

## Crosshair placement

Detectors: dry_peek, opening_duel.
Keep the crosshair at head height where an enemy would appear. Good crosshair placement means little or no correction is needed when an enemy peeks, which wins more duels than fast flicks.

## Communication

Detectors: untraded_death, late_rotation.
Short, fast calls (how many enemies, where, how much damage was done) let teammates trade, rotate and retake in time. A death with no call gives the team no information for the rest of the round.
